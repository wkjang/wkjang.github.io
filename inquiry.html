Scientific Inquiry from Process Data

Clicks and saves in a science simulation app are more than outcomes. I parse raw event logs into ordered inquiry steps, build weighted transition graphs between them, and cluster learners by how they move through hypothesis, experiment, and analysis.

Research question
How do children explore, reason, and make meaning with digital tools?
Data
Digital learning traces with observation and children's work
Contexts
Digital learning at school and at home
Approach
Process analytics with critical digital literacy

In depth

Digital literacies and interaction logs

This strand examines how children navigate, interpret, and construct meaning with interactive technologies. Interaction-log analysis shows how learners explore digital simulations, test hypotheses, interpret feedback, and revise their understanding.

In early literacy contexts, logs can capture how children move through interactive picture books, revisit illustrations, use audio narration and vocabulary supports, and create or revise digital stories. These traces reveal how children engage with multimodal texts and exercise agency.

Clicks are never treated as direct indicators of learning. Log analysis is combined with observation, children's conversations and artifacts, and educators' interpretations. From a critical digital literacy perspective, the work also asks how interface design, language options, accessibility, and algorithmic recommendations shape whose voices and ways of meaning-making are supported.

Critical, human-centered lens

Who is seen, who decides

Social semiotics

Taps, choices, and revisions in an app are acts of meaning-making with a digital text.

Critical big data & sociotechnical

Logs record only what the interface was designed to capture; interface design, language options, and accessibility shape whose strategies appear.

Human-centered AI

Clicks are never treated as learning on their own; logs are read with observation, children's talk, and teachers' interpretations.

Figures

How it works

From signal to evidence

step 1

Capture

Every action in the simulation is logged with a timestamp.

step 2

Parse

Raw events become inquiry steps: instruction and hypothesis, first submission, saving analysis, submitting analysis.

step 3

Graph

Transitions between steps become a weighted, directed graph for each learner.

step 4

Profile

Learners are clustered by their trajectories and compared on learning gains.

Findings

What the analyses show

Process data separate distinct ways of doing inquiry, from targeted exploration and extended analysis to briefer, more exploratory engagement.

Keeping the timing and order of actions shows how learners revise strategies, which completed screens alone do not.
