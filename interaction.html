Caregiver–Child Interaction Dynamics from Pose

Ordinary home video becomes continuous movement and distance signals for both partners. From those signals I build a dependency profile: whether caregiver and child move together, who leads, and whether a child's withdrawal brings the caregiver back.

Research question

In depth

Multimodal meaning-making in real time

Grounded in social semiotics, this work recognizes that early literacies extend beyond spoken and written language to the many ways children communicate, interpret, and represent meaning: through images, gesture, movement, and interaction with people and materials.

Computer vision, natural language processing, head-mounted eye tracking, audio, video, and interaction data are fused to capture what children see, hear, say, and do during shared reading, play, and other literacy-rich activities. Temporal modeling and dynamic interaction graphs show how children coordinate attention, language, gesture, and movement to make meaning as it happens.

During shared reading, for example, a child may look at an illustration, point to a character, respond in a home language, or express an interpretation through drawing. These are interconnected forms of literacy participation that conventional assessments often miss. Ongoing work on embodied learning and caregiver–child proximity develops continuous, time-sensitive measures of these exchanges.

The goal is not to replace human interpretation with automated measurement. Computational analyses are combined with qualitative observation, established assessments, and educators' and families' perspectives to recognize children's diverse ways of making meaning more fully.

How it works

From signal to evidence

step 1

Detect and track

YOLO11-pose finds both people in every frame and ByteTrack keeps caregiver and child identities stable across the session.

step 2

Describe

Each frame yields how much each partner moved and how far apart they are, scaled to body size so camera distance does not matter.

step 3

Couple

Linear, lagged, nonlinear, and rhythm-specific measures test whether the two movement streams rise and fall together, and with what delay.

step 4

Respond

Proximity is coded as approach, stable, or withdraw. The system checks whether a child's withdrawal is followed by a caregiver approach, and which state sequences recur.

Findings

What the analyses show

A single correlation can hide three different organizations: moving in step, one partner leading, or coupling that comes and goes. The dependency profile separates them.

Every statistic is compared against shuffled timing, so a reported dependency is stronger than chance alignment of two active people.

Proximity states extracted from real sessions line up with visibly different moments: withdrawing, approaching in contact, and staying apart.
