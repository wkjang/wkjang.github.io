MOSAIC: Multimodal Observation of Situated Activity, Interaction, and Child Learning

MOSAIC is the framework that ties my research together. It brings multimodal AI, dynamic developmental modeling, and human-centered measurement to bear on how developmental experiences unfold across relationships, contexts, and time. Communication, gaze, gesture, movement, objects, and social response are aligned on one timeline, and the unit of analysis is a situated opportunity: a child's contribution, the response it receives, and what the child does next.

Research question
Do children who share the same setting experience systematically different opportunities to participate, be heard, and learn?
Data
Synchronized video, audio, eye tracking, and human event codes
Unit of analysis
Situated opportunity, nested in child, classroom, and program

In depth

Human-centered AI for authentic assessment and teacher learning

MOSAIC integrates multimodal AI and human-centered inquiry to understand how children construct meaning through language, images, gesture, movement, and interactions with people and materials across homes, classrooms, libraries, and communities.

The Smart Literacy initiatives translate these methods into tools for authentic literacy assessment and teacher learning, supported by a University of Oklahoma seed award for multimodal digital assessment and a faculty grant for AI-supported professional learning. The goal is not a dashboard that assigns a literacy label to a child, but interpretable tools that prompt educators to revisit a recording, recognize an overlooked contribution, compare an automated summary with their own observations, or adapt a text or discussion prompt.

In teacher education, AI-enhanced activities built around classroom discourse, interaction logs, and video help future teachers examine feedback, differentiation, and equitable participation, and critically compare AI interpretations with transcripts, children's work, and their own judgment.

Through mixed-methods validation and human–AI collaboration, the work examines bias, accessibility, consent, privacy, and the consequences of errors, with educators and families as partners in designing and evaluating tools. AI should augment professional reasoning and relationships, not replace either.

Social semiotics

MOSAIC treats language, image, gesture, gaze, and movement as one integrated system of meaning-making.

Critical big data & sociotechnical

Every construct in MOSAIC (initiation, response, continuity, agency) is a choice; each is defined with educators and tested for bias across children, languages, and settings.

Human-centered AI

Tools prompt educators to revisit and reinterpret moments; outputs never assign a literacy label to a child or evaluate a teacher.

Dimensions

Four dimensions of a learning opportunity

dimension 1

Epistemic initiation

Noticing, questioning, predicting, explaining, and representing ideas.

dimension 2

Contingent response

Uptake, elaboration, revoicing, clarification, and transfer of control.

dimension 3

Continuity and revision

Persistence, testing, evidence use, uncertainty, and conceptual change.

dimension 4

Participatory agency

Access to materials, choice, help seeking, and influence over what happens next.

Measurement commitments

Interpretable, theory-grounded, and tested

Measures should capture not only what people do but how they construct meaning and coordinate relationships, grounded in developmental, sociolinguistic, and social-semiotic theory.

Computational measures are tested for cross-context validity, measurement invariance, and algorithmic bias before they are interpreted.

Each new measure has to show what it adds beyond established observational approaches. Human observation anchors interpretation; models extend it.
