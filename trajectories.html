Family, Organizational, and Community Systems

Interactions are embedded in interconnected family, organizational, and community systems. With Head Start programs and other family-serving organizations, I study relational networks, workforce stability, organizational climate, family–professional partnerships, and institutional resources, and how they shape developmental trajectories. Longitudinal, multilevel, and Bayesian models and social network analysis turn long-term partnership data into interpretable evidence for decisions about service quality and family support.

Research question
How do family, program, and workforce systems shape children's developmental opportunity?
Data
Longitudinal child, family, classroom, and workforce data from Head Start and Educare partnerships
Unit of analysis
Child, teacher, classroom, and program
Methods
Longitudinal, multilevel and Bayesian models, social networks, cluster RCT

In depth

Relationships, systems, and development over time

Children's meaning-making unfolds across relationships, learning environments, and longer developmental and institutional timescales.

A U.S. Department of Health and Human Services grant supports social network analysis of how peer networks, language diversity, and classroom social dynamics shape children's opportunities for participation and shared meaning-making in Head Start, including how peer continuity and clustering influence learning over time.

Longitudinal work examines how family and child characteristics relate to reading milestones across development, and how early language and literacy experiences relate to science learning from kindergarten through fifth grade, positioning literacy as a foundation for disciplinary meaning-making.

Together these studies connect moment-to-moment interaction with longer-term trajectories and the family, community, and institutional contexts that shape children's opportunities.

Critical, human-centered lens

Who is seen, who decides

Social semiotics

Peer networks and family routines are social contexts where meanings are shared and negotiated.

Critical big data & sociotechnical

Administrative and survey data under-count some families; models report who is missing and how missingness may bias estimates.

Human-centered AI

Findings are interpreted with programs and families to inform supports, not to rank children, families, or classrooms.

How it works

From signal to evidence

step 1

Developmental trajectories

Longitudinal models follow children over time to see when and for whom gaps in reading and science open or close.

step 2

Nested data

Multilevel and hierarchical Bayesian models account for children within classrooms and programs.

step 3

Peers and networks

Peer-effect and social network models capture how classmates and relationships shape learning.

step 4

Workforce

Latent profiles and longitudinal models track teacher well-being, working conditions, and turnover.

Findings

What the analyses show

Workforce stability matters for children: within-year teacher turnover in Early Head Start and Head Start is linked to children's developmental outcomes.

Teachers' working conditions, whole well-being, and organizational climate shape the quality of their relationships with children.

Language and literacy skills shape science learning from kindergarten to 5th grade and can help close gender, racial/ethnic, and socioeconomic gaps.

Center-based early childhood programs relate to when children with disabilities close science achievement gaps.

Culturally and linguistically diverse families, including refugee families, meet distinct technological barriers and opportunities that shape participation and well-being.
